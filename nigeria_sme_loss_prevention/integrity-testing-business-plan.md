# Business Idea: Cash-Handling Integrity Testing (Prevention & Coaching Model)

## 1. The Original Idea

A service where businesses hire you to test whether employees would engage in shady dealings or theft, similar to mystery shopping.

**Assessment:** This is a real, proven niche, usually called "integrity shopping" or "honesty shopping." Demand exists, but the original framing carried legal and reputational risk.

### Why it can work
- Retail, restaurants, bars, and cash-heavy businesses lose a lot to internal theft.
- Existing mystery-shopping and loss-prevention firms already offer it, which proves demand.
- Startup costs are low. Your main assets are trained testers and good reporting.

### Challenges
- **Competition:** Established loss-prevention firms. Differentiate with a niche (independent restaurants, bars, dispensaries).
- **Entrapment and fairness:** Private employers aren't bound by entrapment law like police, but pressuring someone into misconduct can hurt a client in a wrongful-termination dispute. Design tests around *opportunities* (being undercharged, a skipped receipt), not pressure.
- **Legal variation:** Recording, employee notice, and union rules vary by state and country. Have a lawyer review your methods.
- **Morale:** If staff find out, trust can suffer. Clients may pair testing with a policy noting that "compliance checks may occur."
- **Liability:** Reports can lead to firings, so accuracy, documentation, and consistent standards matter.

---

## 2. Decision: Prevention & Coaching Instead of "Catching Thieves"

You chose to move away from the more dangerous original framing and sell **prevention and coaching**. Testing stays as the core service; what changes is what you do with the results.

**Why this is better:**
- Easier to sell and easier to defend legally.
- Better recurring revenue: clients pay to track improvement rather than hiring you once to catch someone.
- Clients often value "your staff passed 80% of tests, here are your process gaps" more than a list of people to fire.

### Service structure
1. **Baseline audit:** Run a round of tests to find gaps in cash handling and procedures.
2. **Report on process, not people:** Show which situations staff handle well or badly (undercharges, unrung sales, discount abuse), not a firing list.
3. **Training and fixes:** Recommend tighter procedures such as receipt policies, till counts, and camera placement.
4. **Follow-up testing:** Re-test quarterly to show improvement. This is the recurring revenue.

### Starting niche
Independent restaurants or bars, where owners are hands-on and often lose the most to skimming. Start with a few friendly clients at a discount, collect results, and use the case studies to sell the next ones.

## 4. Sample Test Scenarios (Opportunity-Based)

- **Undercharge:** The bill comes out lower than it should. Does the employee flag it or stay quiet?
- **Skipped receipt:** Does the employee offer a receipt and ring the sale up properly?
- **Exact change:** Is the sale rung in before the cash goes in the till?
- **Wrong change:** Does the employee give back too much change, and is the tester's correction noticed?
- **Discount handling:** Are discounts or comps applied only according to policy?
- **Voids and refunds:** Does the employee follow the approval steps?

Each scenario needs a written script, a clear pass/fail standard, and a same-day log of the result. Consistency is what makes your reports credible if a client acts on them.

## 5. Client Pitch

### The 30-second pitch
"Most owners lose 1–3% of revenue to small leaks at the till, like unrung sales, undercharges, and discount abuse, and never see it happening. I run discreet, scripted test visits that show exactly where your cash-handling process has gaps. You get a plain report and fixes, and we re-test quarterly to show the leaks closing."

### Opening message to a prospect
"Hi [Name], I help independent restaurants find where money is slipping through cash handling, without turning it into a witch hunt. I'd like to run a free 3-visit sample audit at [Business] so you can see what the report looks like. Would you be open to a 15-minute chat this week?"

### Objections you'll hear
- **"I trust my staff."** → "Most of the gaps are process problems, not bad people. This shows whether your systems make honesty easy."
- **"Will my staff find out?"** → "Tests are discreet, and results focus on procedures, not naming individuals."
- **"Is it worth it?"** → "If you lose even 1% of $500k in sales, that's $5,000 a year, so the audit pays for itself."

### Tips
- Lead with the free or discounted sample, since it removes the risk for the client.
- Always say "process," never "catching thieves."
- Have clients sign an agreement covering the testing and how results will be used. It protects both sides.

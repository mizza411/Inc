# FMCG 14-day cash test

**Status:** not started  
**Added:** 28 October 2026  
**Shape:** trading only. Buy small packs and resell them. No factory, no new brand, and no software app.

This file is the chat test from 28 October 2026. It is not a started business. Moving it to `Started-Businesses/` needs an explicit yes.

Survey naira figures below are public 2023/24 averages. They are not prices to charge, and they are not a budget.

## What to sell

Four small packs only. Shops already sell these sizes.

1. **Laundry detergent, smallest pack of a brand kiosks already trust.** This is the line that best reduces the risks in the Main risks section. NielsenIQ ranks detergent 7th of the ten categories that make up 64% of Nigeria FMCG sales. A shopper-scan ranking for the 12 months to May 2026 put Viva laundry detergent 3rd by how often people bought it. Viva is an example of a known pack, not a brand to copy. NBS General Household Survey, Wave 5, 2023/24: soap and washing powder were bought by 91.1% of households in the 30 days before the post-harvest visit. That was the most common non-food purchase in that survey. Average spend in that window was about N2,037 in 2023/24 survey naira.

2. **Seasoning, small cubes or tiny sachets.** NBS: spices and condiments were eaten by 98.4% of households after planting and 97.5% after harvest. Households use spices, oils and fats, and vegetables more than five days a week. The scan ranking shows tiny sachets among the packs people actually buy. Resell a pack shops already know.

3. **Cooking oil, smallest bottle or sachet.** NBS: about 9 in 10 households use oils and fats, more than five days a week. NielsenIQ, via BusinessDay on 23 October 2025: almost 6 in 10 shoppers switched brand in the past year because of price. Cooking oil, laundry, and toothpaste switched the most. Buy one cheaper small pack that shops already accept.

4. **Noodles, single pack.** NielsenIQ ranks noodles 9th in that sales list. Biscuits grew faster in the 2025 NielsenIQ list reported by Nairametrics on 19 October 2025 (72.2%). The same article says more people were cutting snacks. Noodles stay on this test because they are a meal.

Sugar stays off this test. In the same NBS survey it had the lowest food spend and the biggest fall in how many households used it. A bag of rice is the highest food spend, and it waits, because a jump in major food prices was the most common shock (71% of households, 75.5% in urban areas) and a bag locks cash. Beer and spirits lead sales value, and they wait, because a first test would add licensing and age checks.

## Who buys

Households you can reach on foot, plus 5 to 10 kiosks. The scan ranking says neighbourhood kiosks, open markets, and roadside sellers are where these packs are bought.

## Why the cash can come more often

Seasoning and oil are used most days. Soap and washing powder were a 30-day purchase for 91.1% of households. An IT contract can pay once and then stop. These packs are bought again because they get used up.

Q1 2026, Worldpanel by Numerator: unit growth was above 8%, and naira sales growth was under 1%. Cash follows packs that leave your hands. NielsenIQ value growth of 54.1% for 2025 sat next to volume growth of only 5.4%. The big percent is not the profit on a pack.

## Before day 1

- Buy only laundry detergent, seasoning, cooking oil, and noodles.
- Buy only the smallest pack shops already sell.
- Buy only what fits at home.
- Pay the wholesaler before you leave.
- Write the buy price of each pack on the card.
- Pick the households and the 5 to 10 kiosks before the first sale.
- Take payment the same day. Cash counts. A transfer that arrives the same day counts. A pack taken today and paid next week does not count as a sale on this test.

## Daily card

Fill one card each evening. Day number runs from 1 to 14.

```
DAY __ of 14
                Buy   Home  Shop  Left  Bad   Same-day money
Detergent       __    __    __    __    __    ______
Seasoning       __    __    __    __    __    ______
Cooking oil     __    __    __    __    __    ______
Noodles         __    __    __    __    __    ______
Transport today ______
Pay-later asked (no goods handed over)  yes / no
```

Buy means packs added that day. Home means packs sold to households. Shop means packs sold to kiosks. Left means packs still with you. Bad means refused, broken, leaked, or expired. Same-day money is what you actually received for that line today.

## Day 14

A line passes when packs left are under half of packs bought for that line, and the money received for it can pay the wholesaler for another lot of the same pack after that day's transport.

- Detergent pass / fail
- Seasoning pass / fail
- Cooking oil pass / fail
- Noodles pass / fail

The test passes when at least 3 lines pass, and no pack left on a promise of later payment. A line that fails is dropped next time. The test fails when buyers will take the goods only on credit, or when transport eats the gap between buy price and sell price.

If the test passes, bottled water is the next lot, as its own test. Mineral water is 6th in the NielsenIQ sales list, and Mr V bottled water was 2nd by scan frequency. Water stays off this card. It is heavy, so it is a second test.

## Main risks

Each risk below is from the public sources at the end of this file. The detergent line reduces these risks when the pack is one shops already trust, in the smallest size. An unknown detergent bag does not get that reduction.

### Food prices and smaller portions

71% of households had been hit by a jump in major food prices (75.5% in urban areas). Almost half, 48.8%, coped by eating less. A dear food lot can meet buyers who are cutting portions.

Detergent reduces this risk because it sits outside that food basket. The same NBS survey still found soap and washing powder bought by 91.1% of households in a 30-day window. Cooking oil and noodles stay on the card, and they still carry this food risk. Detergent is the line that does not depend on people cooking a full pot.

### Brand switching

Almost 6 in 10 shoppers switched brand in the past year because of price. Switching was highest on cooking oil, laundry, and toothpaste. 99% of shoppers still say brand trust matters. An unknown pack can sit on the shelf even when the category sells.

Detergent reduces this risk only when you resell a pack people already buy. The scan ranking put a known laundry detergent 3rd by purchase frequency. Oil carries the same switch risk and also the food-price risk. A known detergent pack avoids the unknown-bag problem. A no-name detergent does not.

### A wholesale rise may not pass through

In Q1 2026, FMCG value growth was under 1% while unit growth was above 8%. A higher buy price may not become a higher sell price.

Detergent reduces this risk because the test uses the smallest pack, so less cash sits in one unit than in a bottle of oil. The sale is a unit leaving your hands. The test does not need the sell price to climb.

### Personal care was the weak quarter

Worldpanel reported that food and beverages still carried Q1 2026 growth, and personal care lagged. Cosmetics would add that weak quarter on top of the other risks.

Detergent reduces this risk because it is a wash product. In the NielsenIQ notes, home essentials such as soap and cleaning rose from 30% to 39% of people spending more on them. The card keeps detergent with food, and it leaves cosmetics off.

### Same-day money can stay small

Frequent small sales are the steadier pattern next to an IT contract that pays once and then stops. Each sale can still be small.

Picking detergent does not remove this. It lowers the chance that the sale vanishes when food prices jump or when buyers cut portions. The day-14 rule still asks whether the money back can buy the next small lot after transport.

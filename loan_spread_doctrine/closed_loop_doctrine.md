# Closed-loop doctrine

Operator playbook: use temporary capital, beat its cost, **repay**, keep the difference. v1 is this text — not a product.

---

## Seats

See also the table in `README.md`. In one line:

- **Consumer** — you pay interest so you can spend now.
- **Worker + pension** — you sell years of hours; the coupon arrives late and often shrinks with naira inflation.
- **Operator** — you rent capital for a **dated** campaign; the campaign must throw off more cash than the rent.
- **Lender** — you *collect* the rent (interest, fee, deposit float).

If a deal puts you in the consumer seat, it is not this doctrine.

---

## Closed loop

**This is the default** (what you asked for).

1. Take a loan **or** take supplier time (goods now, pay later).
2. Put that capacity into something with a **known** way to become cash.
3. The cash you get must cover **principal + interest + fees + tax**, with a remainder.
4. **Pay off** on time.
5. **Keep** only what is left. The campaign has an **ending**.

A loan does not create profit. It only **scales** a spread that already exists.

Call the cost of money **C** (interest, fees, idle days). Call the realized cash return **R**. You keep roughly **R − C**, only if **R** is actually in hand on repay day.

If **R** is not clearly above **C**, do not name it “loan to invest.”

---

## Open loop vs recycle

These are **not** the default. They are named so you do not mix them up.

| Pattern | What you do | Why it exists | Risk |
|---------|-------------|---------------|------|
| **Closed loop** (default) | Repay, pocket, stop | Sleep; a lump; machine off | You stop compounding |
| **Open loop** | Do **not** repay while **R > C**; keep renting the money | Banks and some landlords live here | Miss a payment → asset at risk |
| **Recycle** | Closed loop, then borrow (or draw) again for the next dated campaign — or use the last remainder as the next down payment | Repeat without living in debt | Each cycle still needs **R > C** on a clock |

Open loop is how institutions stay on the interest side. You asked for a campaign **with an ending**. Use open loop only if you later choose that risk on purpose.

## Variants

Same loop, different landlord.

| Variant | You rent from | You “invest” in | You repay with | You keep |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|----------------|----------|
| **Bank loan** | A lender at a stated rate | An asset or a deal | Sale proceeds or saved cash | Exit minus loan minus **C** |
| **Supplier float** | The supplier (time, not a bank) | Stock | Customer cash | Markup |
| **Bridge** | A short facility or a partner | Control of a deal for days/weeks | The next buyer or next party | Fee or assignment margin |
| **Cheap bucket** | A concessional line (e.g. NHF / some DFI windows) **only if you truly qualify** | Something that yields more than *that* cheap rate | Rent or sale | Spread vs the cheap rate — not vs a 24% salary loan |
| **Invert** | Nobody — *you* are the coupon | Someone else’s urgency | They pay you | Interest, fee, or float |

All of these are: **temporary capacity → difference → settle → keep the difference.**

## Nigeria rate buckets

**Illustrative only.** Not your personal offer. Not live quotes. Re-check before a real facility. Figures below match public ranges already used in Inc Abuja research (commercial vs NHF/FMBN), plus operator common sense for trade credit.

| Bucket | Typical shape (illustrative) | When it can be operator fuel | When it is a trap |
|--------|------------------------------|------------------------------|-------------------|
| **Commercial bank / mortgage** | Often **teens to mid-20s % a year** once fees sit in (Abuja research cites **15–25%** commercial mortgages) | Only if the deal’s **cash** yield clearly beats that **C** on the **same calendar** | Buy-to-hold when rent is **10–15% gross** against **15–25%** debt |
| **Concessional (e.g. NHF / FMBN)** | Research cites about **7–10%** **if eligible** | Possible positive carry vs some rental yields — **qualification is the tactic** | Pretending you have this rate when you do not |
| **Supplier time** | **0%** if you pay inside terms; expensive if you miss and pay penalties | Classic trader loop: sell cash, pay later | Treating overdue supplier debt as “free money” |
| **Consumer / salary / card** | Usually among the **dearest** naira | Almost never for this doctrine | Calling it “leverage” |

Banks will not usually lend you money at their lending rate so you can buy the obvious safe paper they already own. Leftover spreads are **operational**: price, speed, access, skill — then capital repeats them.

## Pension vs spread

A **pension** is a delayed coupon on hours you already sold. You do not control the clock well. Inflation and naira can shrink what arrives.

This doctrine is the other chair: you try to **own the spread** on a campaign you can **date** — borrow, beat **C**, repay, keep. That is not “never work.” It is **front-loaded work** to find a real **R > C**, then a **short** capital cycle instead of a **life** of selling hours.

If you cannot name **C**, **R**, the **repay date**, and **cash on that date**, you are still in the pension story, just with extra anxiety.
